Hint book · Chapter 1

What it is

Game Library Manager puts the games you own in different stores into one library. The same game bought on two stores is one record. You add your own backlog status to each game.

It is a desktop app for Windows, Linux and macOS. Version 0.14.0. It is built with Tauri 2, Rust and React. It never downloads or launches games. It is a library, not a launcher.

The Today screen proposes what to play next. The library has two view modes: a cover wall and a table. The theme is Light, Dark or System, and you select one of eleven skins or write your own.

Chapter 2

The three shops

  • Steam uses its official API. You give it your own API key.
  • GOG and Epic have no public API. You log in to them on their own login pages.
  • Epic uses its launcher's private API. It can break any day.
  • Each store has its own switch. If one breaks, you switch it off. Its games stay in your library with your notes and your status. Only you decide to switch a store off.

Chapter 3

Local-first, and private

  • All data lives in one SQLite file on your machine.
  • No server. No accounts.
  • No store credential leaves your computer. Credentials live in the system keyring, or in an encrypted file if there is no keyring.
  • The app never asks for a store password. You log in on the store's own page.
  • You can export your data. You can disconnect a store and keep what you wrote: the records, the links and your status stay.

Chapter 4

Why a desktop app, and not a website

Store logins can only be defended on your own machine. A server that keeps the tokens would break the terms of the stores. It would be an attack target. One IP ban would stop everyone.

Playnite, Heroic and Lutris are desktop apps for the same reason.

Chapter 5

Optional extras

  • IGDB: covers, metadata, and time to beat (main story, main plus extras, completionist), as players report it to IGDB. Without IGDB, everything still works, dedup included.
  • IsThereAnyDeal: wishlist prices. You need a free ITAD key and your country. You see the current price, the store that sells it and the historical lowest price. The list is sorted by discount, and you can filter it and sort it by any column. You can also add wishes by hand, one for each device.
  • Amazon Luna: no credential needed. A wish that is in the Prime Luna catalogue says On Luna. Play it today without buying it.
  • Steam Family: a wish of any store, or a wish added by hand, says Steam Family with the names of the owners when a member of your Steam family owns the game and can lend it. You sign in on Steam's own page. The session stays in the app.

Chapter 6

Dialogue

Choose a question. The answer opens below it.

  1. 1. Does it launch my games?

    No. It never downloads or launches games. It is a library, not a launcher.

  2. 2. Do I need an account?

    No. There are no accounts and no server.

  3. 3. Will it ask for my Steam, GOG or Epic password?

    No. The app never asks for a store password. Steam uses its official API with your own key. For GOG and Epic you log in on their own pages.

  4. 4. What happens when a store breaks?

    You switch that store off, with its own switch. The other stores keep working. Its games stay in your library, with your notes and status.

  5. 5. Do I need IGDB or IsThereAnyDeal?

    No. Both are optional. Without them, everything still works, including dedup.

  6. 6. Why is it not a website?

    Store logins can only be defended on your own machine. See chapter 4.

  7. 7. Where is my data?

    In one SQLite file on your machine. You can export it at any time.

  8. 8. Under which licence is it?

    GPL-3.0-or-later. You can read the code, change it and share it under that licence.

  9. 9. Can I build it myself?

    Yes. Run bun install, then bun run tauri dev. The source is on GitHub.
